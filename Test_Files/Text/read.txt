A car (or automobile) is a wheeled motor vehicle designed primarily to run on roads, seat one to eight people, and transport passengers rather than heavy goods.

Key Characteristics

• Wheels and Structure: Typically features four wheels, a structural chassis, suspension systems, and an enclosed passenger cabin.
• Power Source: Propelled by an internal combustion engine (running on gasoline, diesel, or alternative fuels), an electric battery motor, or a hybrid combination.
• Common Body Styles: Includes sedans, hatchbacks, SUVs, coupes, and wagons, each built for different space, performance, or utility needs.

Core Components

• Engine or Motor: Generates the mechanical or electrical power needed for movement.
• Transmission and Drivetrain: Transfers power from the engine to the wheels (available in front-wheel, rear-wheel, all-wheel, or four-wheel drive).
• Controls and Safety: Features a steering wheel, accelerator, brakes (often with Anti-lock Braking Systems), and lighting.

Brief History

• Birth of the Automobile: Credited to German inventor Karl Benz in 1886 with his Benz Patent-Motorwagen.
• Mass Production: Popularized early on by vehicles like the Ford Model T in 1908, which utilized a moving assembly line.
